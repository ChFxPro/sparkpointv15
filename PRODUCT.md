# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Three audiences, served in this order of frequency:

- **Residents and neighbors** in Transylvania County and the wider WNC region. They arrive from a flyer, a friend, a partner's link, or a search, often on a phone. They want to know what is happening, whether it is for them, and how to show up.
- **Partners and funders**: local organizations, sponsors, foundations, and county/municipal partners. They are judging whether SparkPoint is credible, well-connected, and effective before they commit money, staff time, or their name.
- **Volunteers and donors**: individuals who already like what they see and want a clear, low-friction way to give time or money.

Press and civic leaders are served by `/press` and `/impact`; the homepage should route them there quickly but is not built around them.

## Product Purpose
SparkPoint is a convener and connector. It builds the county's social infrastructure: it brings residents, organizations, schools, and government into relationship, listens through story collection, and connects unmet needs to the right partner. The site makes that work visible, invites people into it, and earns the trust that keeps partners and funders involved.

Success for the homepage: a first-time visitor understands within one screen what SparkPoint does and that it is local, finds the door that fits them (attend, volunteer, partner, give), and leaves trusting that SparkPoint is real, active, and accountable.

## Brand Personality
**Warm, local, bold.** Human and neighborly at heart: real people, real places in Transylvania County, plain-spoken copy. The brand colours (spark pink, gold, purple, orange) and the sparkmark are used with confidence at key moments, not sprayed evenly across every section. Emotional goals: belonging ("this is for me"), energy ("something good is happening here"), and trust ("these people follow through").

## Anti-references
- **Generic nonprofit template**: drone-landscape hero with a centred slogan, rows of icon cards, "Learn More" buttons everywhere, copy interchangeable with any charity.
- **Corporate / SaaS polish**: big metric tiles, gradient-and-glass cards, startup-style feature grids.
- **Clinical or service-agency tone**: anything implying SparkPoint provides crisis, clinical, or medical intervention. It notices and connects to named partners (for example, TC Strong) instead.
- **Cluttered and overwhelming**: many sections all at the same volume, with nowhere for the eye to rest.

## Design Principles
1. **Show the people, not the landscape.** Real faces, rooms, and moments from SparkPoint's own work outrank scenery and illustration as proof that something is happening here.
2. **One door per visitor.** Each audience should find its own path in a glance. Fewer, clearer choices beat many equal ones.
3. **Earn the bold.** Save saturated colour, big type, and motion for the moments that carry meaning (the current event, the ask, the one number that matters), so they land.
4. **Convener, not center.** Credit partners, route to specialists, and frame SparkPoint as the connective tissue, never the hero of every story.
5. **Proof over claims.** Specific dates, names, places, and numbers beat abstractions like "care, trust, and action."

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Many visitors are on phones, often outdoors or on rural connections, so performance and legible contrast over photography matter. Every animation respects `prefers-reduced-motion`; moving media has a visible pause control (WCAG 2.2.2); content is never hidden behind a reveal animation. Plain language at roughly an 8th-grade reading level for resident-facing copy.
